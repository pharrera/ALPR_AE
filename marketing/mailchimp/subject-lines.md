# Subject Lines & Preview Text — Sept 2026 Sends

Three sends, each with a recommended pair plus alternates for A/B testing.

**Two rules these follow.** Subject and preview text never repeat each other — the preview
carries what the subject had no room for. And subjects stay at or under ~45 characters, which
is roughly what an iPhone shows in portrait; anything longer gets cut mid-thought.

**One caution on the churn number.** Mailchimp's read on this account is 42.5% list churn
against a 13.3% peer median. That argues against curiosity-gap subjects that overpromise —
people unsubscribe when the email doesn't match what the subject implied. Every recommendation
below says plainly what's inside.

---

## Thu 9/10 — the roundup

**Recommended**

> **Subject:** Today at 4 PM, a new AI series, and Tech Week `45`
> **Preview:** Contracting readiness is this afternoon. A three-part AI series starts 9/15, and Tech Week sponsorships are open.

The hardest of the three to write, because six sections have no single hook. This one leads
with the only thing that expires today and lets the preview carry the rest. Your original —
*Upcoming Opportunities & LB Tech Week 2026* — is accurate but gives a reader no reason to
open now.

**Alternates**

| Subject | Len | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Contracting starts at 4 PM — plus what's next | 45 | Urgency |
| Upcoming Opportunities & LB Tech Week 2026 | 42 | Original |
| Meet the 2026 Summit honorees | 29 | Curiosity |

The honorees subject will likely win on opens and lose on clicks — the honorees sit fourth in
the email, so anyone opening for them scrolls past three other asks first. Worth testing only
if you move that section up.

## Mon 9/14 — Session 1 last call

**Recommended**

> **Subject:** Tomorrow at 10 AM: Purpose, People, and AI `42`
> **Preview:** Ninety minutes on Zoom with Rene Redwood. Session 1 of three, and there's still room.

The strongest of the three sends. One deadline, one action, and the subject states both the
timing and the substance. The preview adds the facilitator, the format, and a soft
availability cue.

**Alternates**

| Subject | Len | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Last call — Session 1 is tomorrow morning | 41 | Urgency |
| 90 minutes tomorrow to sharpen your business | 44 | Benefit |
| Starts tomorrow: Building an AI-Ready Business | 46 | Specific — may truncate |

If you A/B one send this month, make it this one: a last-call email has a clean, measurable
outcome, and what you learn transfers to Sessions 2 and 3.

## Fri 9/18 — Tech Week sponsorship

**Recommended**

> **Subject:** Ten days to Tech Week — sponsorships are open `45`
> **Preview:** Sept 28 – Oct 1. Sponsors join founders, investors, and civic leaders — and sit in the room for the Summit.

Different audience from the other two: organizations deciding on spend, not owners deciding on
a morning. The countdown creates the deadline, and the preview names who else is in the room,
which is the actual argument for sponsoring.

**Alternates**

| Subject | Len | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Put your brand at the center of LB Tech Week | 44 | Benefit |
| Sponsor Long Beach Tech Week 2026 | 33 | Original — plain |
| Meet the 2026 Summit honorees | 29 | Curiosity |

*Sponsor Long Beach Tech Week 2026* is a label rather than a reason. It works if this list
already knows Tech Week well; the countdown version works better if they don't.

## Applying these in Mailchimp

Each template already carries its preview text as a hidden preheader in the HTML. Mailchimp
also offers its own preview text field, and **when both are set, some clients show them
back to back.** Pick one:

- **Leave Mailchimp's field blank** — the HTML preheader is used. Simplest, and it's already
  written.
- **Set Mailchimp's field** — it takes precedence. Use this if you pick a preview line
  different from the one baked into the template.

If you choose different preview text than what's in a template, say which and I'll update the
HTML so the two can't drift apart.
